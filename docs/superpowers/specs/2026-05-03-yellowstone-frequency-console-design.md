# Yellowstone Frequency Console Design

## Status

Approved for specification. Do not implement until the user reviews this document and confirms the next step.

## Product Goal

The atlas should stop feeling like a web page that lists sounds. It should become a future natural radio for Yellowstone: a warm mineral-metal console that lets people tune into living environmental frequencies.

The user is not choosing items from a playlist. They are operating a physical-feeling instrument that receives Yellowstone's natural channels.

## Design Thesis

Use a believable object in the foreground and an expressive ecological signal field in the background.

- Foreground: a wide, low, matte mineral-metal radio console with real controls.
- Background: a slow, breathing life-frequency field generated by the current sound.
- Interaction: channel keys define the listening range, a large mechanical knob tunes inside that range, and playback modes decide how the console continues.
- Tone: premium, tactile, futuristic, and natural. Avoid generic cyberpunk, neon dashboards, flat playlist UI, and purely decorative waveform effects.

## Core Object

The central object is a wide, flat control console, closer to high-end audio hardware or a field research instrument than a nostalgic radio.

### Physical Layout

- Left side: embedded information screen.
- Center: low metal speaker grille or acoustic vent.
- Right side: large tuning knob with mechanical tick marks and nearby channel keys.
- Lower strip: playback progress, queue position, channel range, and mode state.

### Material Language

- Main body: warm gray mineral metal, matte, thick, and low-gloss.
- Secondary materials: dark rubber, ceramic white, subtle brushed metal.
- Accent details: restrained copper, sulfur yellow, oxidized blue, or mineral green.
- Controls: mechanical, tactile, precise. Knobs and buttons should look pressable or turnable.
- The device must feel expensive and engineered, not toy-like or generic.

## Frequency Channels

Use natural frequency channels instead of ordinary content categories.

### AIR

For birds, wind, open meadow, dawn, and high-frequency life.

Visual language:
- feather traces
- flight arcs
- air-current ribbons
- fine high-frequency lines

### HEAT

For geysers, steam vents, thermal basins, and pressure.

Visual language:
- steam columns
- mineral deposits
- pressure rings
- heat-map strata

### WATER

For lake, stream, wetland, ice, and liquid resonance.

Visual language:
- refraction grids
- wave rings
- lake-edge contours
- fluid interference patterns

### ANIMAL

For elk, wolves, mammals, movement, and body presence.

Visual language:
- body silhouettes
- tracks
- low-frequency pulse fields
- herd or pack positioning

### NIGHT

For night ecology, distant calls, darkness, and low-visibility sound.

Visual language:
- dark ecological field
- distant source points
- subdued sonar rings
- hidden animal outlines

### ALL

All is not a natural channel. It is a full-spectrum scan mode.

Visual language:
- layered traces from all channels
- scanning beam or range sweep
- less animal-specific than the focused channels

## Playback Rules

Channel selection defines the playback scope.

- When `AIR` is selected, sequential playback stays inside `AIR`.
- When `HEAT` is selected, sequential playback stays inside `HEAT`.
- When `WATER` is selected, sequential playback stays inside `WATER`.
- When `ANIMAL` is selected, sequential playback stays inside `ANIMAL`.
- When `NIGHT` is selected, sequential playback stays inside `NIGHT`.
- When `ALL` is selected, playback can cross all available sounds.

Playback mode defines behavior inside the current scope.

- `SEQ`: play the current channel scope in order.
- `SHUFFLE`: randomize inside the current channel scope.
- `HOLD`: stop after the current sound.

The large knob always tunes inside the current channel scope. If the user turns the knob during autoplay, manual tuning wins immediately.

## Autoplay Behavior

Autoplay is allowed, but it should not feel like an uncontrolled playlist.

- Pressing play starts the current sound.
- When the sound ends, the console advances according to the selected playback mode.
- In `SEQ`, it advances to the next sound in the current channel.
- In `SHUFFLE`, it picks another sound from the current channel.
- In `HOLD`, it stops on completion.
- In `ALL`, `SEQ` and `SHUFFLE` can move across all sounds.

If a channel has only one sound, `SEQ` and `SHUFFLE` loop that sound and the screen shows `1/1` for that channel.

## Screen Information

The left embedded screen should be small but useful. It should not become a big dashboard.

Required screen fields:

- active channel
- current sound title
- time-of-day or field phase
- zone or habitat
- playback mode
- queue position inside current channel

Optional screen details:

- brief ecological note
- source/credit
- signal strength or frequency code derived from metadata

## Controls

### Channel Keys

Dedicated channel buttons:

- AIR
- HEAT
- WATER
- ANIMAL
- NIGHT
- ALL

The active channel should have a physical pressed or lit state.

### Tuning Knob

The main knob is the emotional center of interaction.

Expected behavior:

- turn right: next sound in the active channel scope
- turn left: previous sound in the active channel scope
- click or press: play/pause

If the visual knob cannot support an accessible click target cleanly, add a separate play/pause control near the knob while keeping the knob dedicated to tuning.

The visual design should imply mechanical resistance through tick marks, knurled edges, pointer indicator, or subtle rotational state.

### Mode Control

Playback mode can be a three-position control:

- SEQ
- SHUFFLE
- HOLD

This should be visually separate from the channel keys, because channel and mode are different concepts.

## Background Life-Frequency Field

The background is the main expressive surface. It appears behind the console after a sound starts or changes.

The background should be led by natural essence, with scientific visualization as a secondary language.

Primary layer:

- animal form
- feather or track pattern
- steam, water, or night ecology shape
- natural silhouette or terrain-like motion

Secondary layer:

- spectrum lines
- sonar rings
- coordinate marks
- particle traces
- subtle technical labels

The first read should be nature. The second read should be instrument analysis.

## Motion Direction

Use slow breathing motion.

- On sound change, the field should bloom or retune from behind the console.
- During playback, it drifts, expands, contracts, or breathes slowly.
- Avoid obvious volume-reactive jumping unless used very sparingly.
- Motion should feel like a living system, not a music visualizer.

Respect reduced motion by minimizing or disabling ambient animation.

## Layout Requirements

- The console should be the central first-viewport object.
- The background field should be visible behind and around the console.
- Controls must be legible and operable on desktop and mobile.
- The layout may scroll on small screens if needed for accessibility, but the primary desktop experience should feel like a single hero instrument rather than a long article.
- Do not reintroduce card grids, route maps, stop cards, generic timeline layouts, or HLE-style tiny CRT as the main object.

## Accessibility

- All buttons need clear accessible names.
- Channel state should use `aria-pressed` or equivalent.
- Playback mode state should be available to assistive technology.
- The current sound and status should be announced with an appropriate live region.
- Keyboard users must be able to change channel, mode, previous/next sound, and play/pause.
- Reduced-motion users should get static or very minimal background fields.

## Testing Expectations

Static tests should verify:

- the console shell exists
- the channel controls exist
- the mode control exists
- the central audio/player control exists
- the background field region exists
- the screen/readout is labelled

Runtime/browser tests should verify:

- selecting a channel filters the active scope
- next/previous stay inside the selected channel
- `ALL` allows cross-channel movement
- `SHUFFLE` stays inside the selected channel
- autoplay advances according to mode
- audio load errors disable playback gracefully
- desktop and mobile layouts do not overlap critical controls

## Non-Goals

- Do not build a generic playlist page.
- Do not use the previous HLE one-screen experiment as the design target.
- Do not use the previous geomorphic rupture layout as the design target.
- Do not make the background a generic waveform visualizer.
- Do not make the screen a dense analytics dashboard.
